## POV Overview

### The Things We Carried in the Dark: A Soldier's Memoir

**Day — the first full day inside walls that are ours. Thirty hours of it I have to take on other people's word.**

I went to sleep on a rock in the shallows with the water talking to itself, and I woke up on a battlefield.

That is not new. That is most nights. What was new is that it went quiet.

I had just finished someone — I remember the give of it in my wrists. Then no sound. No ring of metal, no shouting, none of the muttering the spell-throwers do under their breath. I looked up and my line was gone. Every one of them. Just me in the open with the ground already paid for.

So I turned around to see what was left to kill.

They were children. All of them. Armoured, holding swords too long for their arms, looking up at me.

What I felt was not grief. It was disgust, and it was pointed at me — a grown woman with a body count standing over children with weapons. I dropped the sword. When it hit the dirt I was one of them. Small. And the army in front of me was full-grown again and coming.

I know why my head builds that particular room. It goes back to the doll. Everyone I was ever pointed at was somebody's kid, and I did not have to look at that while I was following orders, and now I cannot stop. There is a struggle between two people, and then there is war, and war is just innocence battling innocence. I did not know I thought that until I said it out loud.

Then [[Jorlan Duskryn]] was there.

He is dead. I know he is dead — I know it the way you know a thing in a dream and keep going anyway. He stood at my shoulder and coached me. Footwork. Angles. Drow work, the close ugly kind I have never been taught and somehow had in my hands. He walked me back through everything I have done, and then through *who told me to do it*, and there they were across the field. My officers. Watching.

That is what lit it. Not the enemy. The men who aimed me.

The sword came back into my hand and would not hold a shape. Short blade. Long. A great axe. A hammer, for a breath. Then it settled as [[Indigo]]'s trident, and I have not decided what to do with that.

I went through them. I do not remember it as fighting; I remember it as work getting done. And then it was quiet again and I was alone again, which is apparently the only ending my head knows how to write.

---

Then I was somewhere else, and did not know I had been anywhere.

[[Blarg]] stood in a wet green valley with mountains at his back and the dark coming for a village that was his. Not dark like night. Dark like something arriving. And there was a light coming *out of him* — mint-coloured, low, from under the skin — pushing back.

Everyone was there. [[Darby Stonefeather]]. [[Amanita de'Champignon]]. [[Aeolus]]. [[Stool]]. Me.

I am not good at the miracle end of things. What I am good at is the thirty seconds before a fight, when you get your breath flat and your hands quiet and you stop being afraid on purpose. So I did that, and then I did it *at* him. Held him steady. That is the whole of what I contributed and I would do it again.

[[Amanita de'Champignon]] put a ring of thinking mushrooms around him. [[Darby Stonefeather]] came in on wings with a cavalry of burning bird-things out of her amulet. And [[Aeolus]] looked at [[Demogorgon]] — the actual thing — and told it to go away. Out loud. And it went.

I have been in the room for a lot of tactics. That was the best one.

---

I woke up wrong.

I know that now. I did not know it then, and that is the part I want written down, because I have been afraid of exactly this since the fish-town.

What I remember is my hands. A dagger and a stone, putting an edge on something already sharp, and going anyway. Oil, stone, cloth, oil. It felt *correct*. It felt like the only correct thing in the world and everything else was noise. I went through stones the way you go through water in a desert.

[[Darby Stonefeather]] came out to my island. She had built something — a house out of fungus, a pen for that spider of hers — and she crossed all the way over to ask me to come see it, in private, because she wanted to talk.

I did not answer her. I heard her. I knew she was there. I did not look up.

She left thinking she had been shut out, and she took it personally, and I could not spare the half a breath it would have cost to tell her she was wrong. I have spent every day since the cages standing between these people and whatever is coming. The first time I was the thing that hurt one of them, I did not even notice it happening.

Then it got worse and quieter. Six hours of it. I stopped sharpening and just stopped. I have seen soldiers go like that after a long push. I have walked past them.

[[Amanita de'Champignon]] came across the stepping stones and sat down next to me. No warning, no preamble. She does not ask permission and I have decided I like that about her.

I said: *I'm here. What?*

She put a hand over me, let the spores go, looked at me the way a field surgeon looks at a wound — and pulled it out of me.

It came back slow. Like the tide coming in under you. A little light, a little more, then focus, and by the time I noticed it had already been happening a while. I did thank her. I got up and left immediately after, so I do not know how much credit that buys.

And with the focus came the rest: the last six hours handed back all at once. [[Darby Stonefeather]] at the edge of my alcove. My own hands. And underneath it, a needle. Something small pushed into me, and everything already wrong in me poured through the hole and came out bigger.

[[Demogorgon]].

He did not make it up. That is the thing I cannot put down. He did not invent a single part of what I felt. He found what was already there and leaned on it.

They wanted me to lie down and take something soft for it. I went for a run instead. Ran the perimeter, counted the ground, marked where the tunnels go. It is the only medicine I have ever trusted.

[[Indigo]] came with me. Of course he did. He watched the whole thing from the water, said nothing about it, gave me that look he gives, and fell into step. And somewhere out on the run he put a sentence in my head without opening his mouth:

*When I fight the Six Fingered man, I want you by my side.*

He did not ask. He assumed. I have spent my life being aimed by people who wanted a weapon, and this is the first time in a long while somebody wanted the person holding it.

---

We went east after that, into the tunnels past the mushrooms. Something out there is wrong in an interesting way — fungus that glows gold and orange and does not answer to [[Amanita de'Champignon]], which I did not know was possible. [[Darby Stonefeather]] walked straight into a web and is still in it. [[Blarg]] is standing in front of a mushroom that looks like the moon, asking it questions.

And out in the dark, a long way off but not far enough to miss, I felt [[Jimjar]]. Him and the grey dwarves with him. Direction and distance, dropped into my head like someone singing it. [[Indigo]] felt it at the same second, and neither of us can explain that.

We can cut them off inside the day if we move.

I still want to hold [[Amanita de'Champignon]]'s warpick. And the trident. I know how that sounds. I am not going to explain it.

## Session Analysis

### Narrative Summary

The session opens above the table, not in the fiction. Addison arrives having read **2024 DMG Chapter 8 — Bastions**, reads the definition aloud twice (*"A bastion is a location that belongs to a player character or a party, a home, a stronghold, and a place of power that the character develops over the course of a campaign"*), and formally grants the party their first bastion. He is openly excited about it — his three-year previous campaign never let the party settle anywhere, and *"every time he gave us a glimpse of it, he pulled it away."* Table flavour runs high through the intro: [[Kit Aluri]] reads the dictionary definition and reports it *"does not help me"*; [[Darby Stonefeather]] cuts through with *"It's a castle"*; Addison quiets the table with *"One, two, three. Eyes on me"* and Austin fires back *"Get that, teacher. It's a Sunday."*

Housekeeping follows. Addison reconciles heroic inspiration (and takes one of [[Aeolus]]'s away for never using them) and reads out the standing madness tally: zero for [[Aeolus]], one each for [[Amanita de'Champignon]], [[Blarg]] and [[Darby Stonefeather]], **two for [[Kit Aluri]]**. He deletes [[Amanita de'Champignon]]'s point outright as a reward for her S23 dream. Anyone who built a drow character for him gets a free skill proficiency or expertise for the session — [[Darby Stonefeather]] takes **animal handling expertise**, [[Blarg]] takes **religion**. [[Kit Aluri]] is offered the same and does not choose one until the very last roll of the night. Addison also dangles a standing riddle about his AI-generated NPC **Dr. Polymer** — one specific unintended detail makes him a future villain — with a *"fairly significant"* prize. **Nobody ever solves it and no prize is awarded.**

The body of the session is **four dream sequences and the party's first Bastion turn.** Addison establishes the terms up front: *"You're not in any initiative, there's not going to be any combat."*

**[[Kit Aluri]]'s dream** is the session's emotional centre. She describes a battlefield that goes silent mid-kill: her own army vanishes, and the enemy she has been slaughtering turns out to be children. She stays an adult — *"which is why she gets this feeling of disgust"* — drops her sword in revulsion, and the moment it hits the ground she becomes a child herself while the enemy reverts to armed adults. Addison stops the session cold to run an **above-table content check** on the imagery; the table consents unanimously and [[Kit Aluri]] confirms *"nothing bad happens."* Asked for the root of it, Taylor delivers the line Addison immediately flags as the quote of the night: the difference between a fight between two people and *"the struggle of war, which is really just innocence battling innocence."* Addison rewards it with a free session-long proficiency and pushes on Kit's age — she lands on **mid-twenties**, and Addison establishes that orcs likely reach adulthood around 12 or 13, meaning she has been fighting since she was a child herself. He also asks, without demanding an answer, whether her old orders came from **the Zhentarim** or from some cruel lord's army. It goes unanswered on purpose.

[[Kit Aluri]] fails her madness saves. Inside the dream, [[Jorlan Duskryn]] appears as a ghost she knows is a ghost — Addison explicitly frames it as the Dexter model, a dead mentor coaching a killer — and teaches her **drow fighting technique she has no waking reason to know**, drawing on a buried memory of watching drow fight. Her shape-shifting greatsword runs through short sword, long sword, great axe and warhammer before settling on **[[Indigo]]'s trident**. [[Jorlan Duskryn]] fans her guilt into rage by reminding her not just what she did but *who ordered it*, and she cleaves through the army — Addison: *"You're a honey badger. You are a honey badger ripping these things to shreds."* Then silence, and she is alone again.

**[[Blarg]]'s dream** runs simultaneously and is the session's biggest lore reveal. He stands above a goblin outpost that looks like the one he abandoned, watching [[Demogorgon]] approach, paralysed. He passes his save, and a wave of power rises **from inside him**. Addison threads the reveal through it: flashes of **faerzress** where it has no business being, then immaculate, well-lit, deeply-hewn stone, warmth, and the sensation of being *"a dwarf baby"* swaddled and carried by the same goblin shaman who raised him — except in this memory **she has dwarven features.** Florian narrates mint-green moonlight pushing back the artificial dark, and — crucially — sees the whole party standing with him, [[Stool]] and [[Indigo]] included, and resolves *"that this time he's not gonna abandon anyone."*

Because [[Blarg]] named [[Demogorgon]] in his own dream, Addison rules he must save again **at disadvantage**, and opens the dream to the whole party the way [[Amanita de'Champignon]]'s was opened in S23. Everyone comes in through a moonlight portal shedding leaves and forest air. Addison sets a **group average DC of 21** to reduce the disadvantage to a flat roll. [[Amanita de'Champignon]] summons a circle of sentient myconids; [[Kit Aluri]] steadies herself and extends the calm to [[Blarg]] (athletics); [[Darby Stonefeather]] erupts glowing orange **axe beak**-headed griffins and hippogriffs from her amulet and flies on celestial Aasimar-style wings; and [[Aeolus]], after trying to refuse to be there at all, simply **screams at [[Demogorgon]] to go away.** [[Echo]] amplifies his voice. Addison is delighted enough to threaten a free bard level — *"Are you gonna intimidate the gosh darn prince of demons?"* — and the group average clears. [[Blarg]] passes both saves at DC 14, describes the moonlight pushing [[Demogorgon]] out of the dream space entirely, and Addison confirms the demon lord's attention had already been drawn by the S21 shrine-reshaping at [[Sloobludop]].

Back in [[Kit Aluri]]'s dream, the bill comes due. She reaches **four levels of madness**, crossing the three-point threshold into her **first level of long-term madness**: thirty hours (d10 × 10, rolled 3) of the DMG p.260 1–10 effect, *compulsive repetition.* Addison makes it specific and cruel — she is doing what she already does, *"just worse and more manically"*: polishing and sharpening, oiling the same blade sixteen times, *"You just went through 17. You're collecting whetstones, Kit."*

The back half is the **first Bastion turn**, run as a montage over roughly the first six hours and then pushed toward the thirty-hour mark. [[Blarg]] wakes first, lays out candles, casts **Ceremony** as flavour for advantage, uses **Guidance** on himself, and prays to **[[Selûne]]** — thanking her for the dream and asking what his mission is. He hits a **30**, exactly Addison's top threshold. The result: a shrine assembles itself in ten to fifteen minutes out of roots and moonlight channelled down through crystals and the **faerzress** in the high ceilings; his infirmary and herb garden are guaranteed by the end of the bastion hour; **[[Topsy]] and [[Turvy]] become more stable**; and **refugee goblinoids — goblins, hobgoblins, bugbears — begin making their way toward the beacon**, overcoming the fear of reprisal that kept the good ones in place before.

[[Darby Stonefeather]] builds a fungal den and stable for [[Roxy]] plus a pen for [[Clementine]], then goes looking for [[Kit Aluri]] with an investigation check and [[Roxy]]'s nose. She finds the island, jumps across, and invites Kit to see the house and talk in private. **Kit does not respond at all.** Addison hands Darby a free insight — she knows Kit is not herself — but Austin plays the hurt straight: *"That's a real big bummer because Darby's gonna take that really personally."* [[Clementine]] gets a size ruling (dog-sized, *"nearly a juvenile"*) and Addison offers to statblock her properly, confirming she has a cantrip and can hide inside a Goliath's front pocket.

[[Amanita de'Champignon]] casts **plant growth (enrichment)** over the fungal food forest and rolls **medicine 25** and **arcana 22**. The medicine result doubles the bastion's food and medicinal stores, anchors the kelp against invasive urchins, binds coral to her mycelial threads, and creates a healing aura strong enough that *"even the common folk would essentially gain the benefits of a long rest over a day, even without resting."* The arcana result buys her one good defensive property: **self-healing walls** — fungus networked through the bastion's walls that bridges and regrows breaches, with spores broadcasting the alarm.

[[Aeolus]] gets the session's comic set-piece. [[Echo]] pecks him awake — *"Wake up, sleepy head. Wake up, you idiot. Everybody's working and you're snoozing and you got wine to grow"* — tries to fly off, and rebounds off an invisible wall fifteen feet out that smells of roses and wine. The raven is revealed as a **raging fae alcoholic**, bound to [[Aeolus]], starving, and bitterly convinced he should be an archfey himself. When he shouts it at the sky, **[[Naos]] laughs inside [[Aeolus]]'s head**: *"Between you and me, he probably should be an archfey."* [[Echo]] eventually strikes the bargain outright — food and cooperation in exchange for stronger wine and stronger power — and flies [[Aeolus]] to [[Amanita de'Champignon]] and [[Darby Stonefeather]] to start the vineyard.

Six hours in, [[Kit Aluri]] has deteriorated from manic sharpening to something Taylor describes as *"a real deep depression"* — curled up, silent, not moving. [[Amanita de'Champignon]]'s spores flag that a companion is wrong; she crosses the tidal stepping stones and sits down beside Kit unannounced. Kit fails both a Wisdom and a Charisma save against Amanita's passive presence (DC 16) and is pulled out far enough to speak: *"I'm here. What?"* A **medicine 20** tells Amanita this is long-term madness lasting under fifty hours, and she casts **Lesser Restoration**, which Addison confirms specifically cures short- or long-term madness. Kit does not resist — she does not see Amanita as a threat and does not think anything is wrong with her. Coming out of it, she gets the last six hours returned as memory and understands for the first time that **[[Demogorgon]] put an access point into her soul and used what was already there** — Addison: *"that little needle, that little access point to your soul... made all of the bad parts worse."* Offered mushrooms and rest, Kit goes for a run and calisthenics instead. [[Indigo]], who watched the whole thing, gives Amanita a smile, a nod and a wink and jogs after Kit, telling her telepathically: *"When I fight the Six Fingered man, the Six Fingered Humanoid, I want you by my side."*

The party then chooses a direction. Addison lays out the compass from the merfolk's own intelligence: **south/left toward [[Sloobludop]]** (left only ~36 hours ago in-fiction, and [[Jimjar]] and the Duergar are still unencountered), **north/right toward [[Menzoberranzan]] or the land route to [[Mantol-Derith]]**, and **east/down into uncharted territory** — where the merfolk warn of a **deep well dropping to the next level of the Underdark**, emanating something *"hostile and dark and abysmal,"* ringed with the bones of small, medium and large beasts. There is also a quarry, a second safer covered well, something *"hot and red"* suggesting lava, and uncharted mushrooms. The party picks the mushrooms and explicitly avoids the dark emanation.

The session ends on a hard cliffhanger. In the mushroom cove, a **homebrew fungus** glows gold and orange at the intersection of **faerzress, primordial and celestial energy, now being warped by fiendish abyssal forces.** [[Amanita de'Champignon]] reads it and hits the wall of her own power: she can learn nothing more and *"can't make it grow faster, slower... can't do anything to it."* Addison flags it as potent, harmful and possibly explosive, requiring a high survival check to harvest safely, and defers the design *"a couple of weeks."* On the final round of perception checks **[[Darby Stonefeather]] rolls a natural 1 and is caught in webbing** with [[Clementine]] — *"now you're stuck right here for next session."* [[Kit Aluri]]'s 24 (proficiency upgraded to expertise on the spot, spending her session bonus at last) gives her and [[Indigo]] a simultaneous divine pull locating **[[Jimjar]] and the Duergar** close enough to intercept within the day. The natural 20s locate an **undiscovered shrine** and Addison confirms *"there's going to be an encounter here"* before cutting. [[Aeolus]] is given the last word and the callback lands: *"go away, Demogorgon."* A final "luck roll" on Sirius's lucky d20 comes up **11** — *"Purely neutral. You get nothing."* Curtain.

### Session Setting

The session begins in the **[[The Starlit Grotto]]**, the merfolk temple-bastion above [[The Mermaid Cove]], during the night's rest the party bedded down for at the end of [[Session 23 — Gods Above, Abyss Below]]. It is the party's **first bastion** and the first home base they have had in the campaign — [[Darby Stonefeather]] names it directly: *"it's kind of the first time we've had like a home base where we can strategize and plan without the threat of. Yeah, danger."*

⚠️ **The bastion's name is unsettled.** Addison calls it *"your Deep Lake Bastion"* at the top of the session, then *"the deep lake or dark lake depths"* mid-session, then *"the dark lake"* near the end. Vault canon has the lake as [[The Darklake]] and the S23 site as **The Starlit Grotto**. Not resolvable from this transcript — see **Continuity Flags**.

**In-game elapsed time:** the session covers the night's dreams plus roughly the first thirty hours of bastion time, pushed as a montage. The only hard anchors given at the table are Addison's *"it was only just, you know, essentially like 36 hours ago that you left"* [[Sloobludop]]; his *"we're probably six hours into the... first three. 6 hours"* marking the point where Kit is found; and the thirty-hour duration of Kit's long-term madness, which Addison explicitly plays out and then jumps past. Addison also floats flashing forward *"at least the five days or a full 10 day."* **In-game days since the start of the story: [Unknown/Ambiguous]** — no day count was stated at the table, consistent with S23.

The dream sequences occupy no measurable Underdark time. Addison hedges their internal chronology deliberately: *"this is maybe a second night or this is somehow the same night time is very strange. You've all seen inception."*

### Themes & Emotional Beats

- **Innocence battling innocence.** The session's thesis, delivered by [[Kit Aluri]] and immediately canonised by Addison as *"that amazing quote."* Her dream literalises it: a battlefield where both sides are children, and where becoming a child herself does not stop the adults from levelling weapons at her. It reframes her entire body count as a structural crime rather than a personal one — and crucially, she indicts **the people who gave the orders** as hard as she indicts herself. *"I don't think she just focuses on her taking the orders, but also the people giving the orders."*
- **The house that madness builds is made of your own furniture.** Addison's most important line about the madness system lands in Kit's cure: [[Demogorgon]] *"made all of the bad parts worse"* through *"that little needle, that little access point to your soul."* Her long-term madness is not a foreign compulsion — it is her own maintenance ritual, the humming-to-her-gear habit, cranked past function into harm. Sixteen oilings. Seventeen whetstones. The horror is that it looks exactly like her.
- **A home for the first time.** [[Darby Stonefeather]] names it: *"it's kind of the first time we've had like a home base where we can strategize and plan without the threat of. Yeah, danger."* Every character's bastion contribution is a statement of what they want to be — [[Blarg]] builds a hospital and a shrine, [[Amanita de'Champignon]] builds food and healing walls, [[Darby Stonefeather]] builds a stable and a pen for her pet, [[Aeolus]] is press-ganged into a vineyard.
- **Belonging that arrives late.** [[Blarg]] discovers he was born a dwarf in a lit, immaculate, deep-carved hall — and that the goblin shaman who raised him was carrying him out of it. His dream resolution is not power but a promise: *"this time he's not gonna abandon anyone."* His shrine then draws the good goblinoids who were *"too afraid of reprisals to even try to escape."* He is building the refuge he did not get.
- **Protection as failure.** Kit's arc has always been standing between people and what is coming. This session she is the harm — she stonewalls [[Darby Stonefeather]], who takes it personally and spends the rest of the session pouting, then gets webbed, then names it: *"Already had a bad day, wasn't able to talk to the Kit like she wanted to, and now is stuck in web."* The friction from S21 deepens without either character addressing it.
- **Being chosen, on purpose.** [[Indigo]] does not ask Kit to fight beside him; he assumes it. Against eleven sessions of Kit being the one who is never handed the vision, the gift or the voice, this is the second consecutive session where something reaches for her specifically — and it costs her nothing and asks nothing but presence.
- **Words as weapons, again.** [[Aeolus]] defeats the Prince of Demons in a dream by yelling at it. Addison's reaction — a threatened free bard level — echoes the S14 beat where Kit resolved a confrontation by talking. The campaign keeps rewarding the party for refusing to make it a fight. *"I mean, why should he fight if he can just tell him to go away?"*
- **The limits of the mushroom girl.** [[Amanita de'Champignon]] has spent the campaign as the one who can always read, grow, calm or connect. The closing fungus is the first thing that shuts her out completely: *"You have no power over this, over this fungus at all. You can't make it grow faster, slower. You can't do anything to it."* Deliberate, and deliberately placed at the cliffhanger.
- **Table culture.** Heavy content handled openly — Addison stops the session cold to run a consent check on the children imagery, and the table answers plainly (*"I consent"* / *"Yeah, definitely. You go for it."*). Kit's stated goal for the night, texted to an absent Matt: *"My new personal goal is to get Addison to cry."* Addison's counter, later: *"Your goal is to make me cry, Taylor."*

### Patterns, Progress & Future Implications

- **The campaign's answer to the madness system is now established.** [[Amanita de'Champignon]]'s **Lesser Restoration** cures short- *and* long-term madness, and Addison read the full cure list aloud on air: calm emotions, lesser restoration, remove curse, dispel evil, greater restoration. Between that and [[Aeolus]]'s calm emotions in S23, the party now has two independent answers. Given that Addison has confirmed the save DC escalates *"every other level"* and rose again after [[Demogorgon]] surfaced, expect madness to remain a recurring tax rather than a one-time crisis.
- **Dream sequences are this campaign's character-development engine, and they have a cost model.** Four sessions running, Addison has used sleep to hand out permanent boons (S23's soul realms, S24's shrine and dwarven-origin reveal) and permanent damage (Kit's madness ladder) in the same breath. The consistent mechanic: **describe your dream honestly and you are rewarded; name a demon lord and you are punished.** [[Blarg]] triggered his own second save by saying "Demogorgon" out loud inside his dream.
- **Group-assist DCs are becoming Addison's default for non-combat stakes.** The average-of-21 mechanic here mirrors the S22–S23 pattern. It reliably produces the party's best moments because it forces every player to describe a contribution in fiction before rolling. Expect it again.
- **[[Blarg]] is being built into the party's institutional anchor.** He is now functionally un-maddenable (+8 Wisdom, two clean DC-14 saves), he owns the only shrine, the only hospital-in-progress, and the only mechanism actively recruiting NPCs to the bastion. His dwarven-origin reveal converts him from "the goblin druid" into someone with a stolen birthright and a debt — and the refugees arriving are the direct narrative payoff of that debt.
- **[[Kit Aluri]]'s isolation arc has flipped into an interdependence arc.** S21 ended with something answering her prayer for the first time. S23 gave her her ghosts at peace. S24 is the first time the party actively **saved her** rather than the reverse — and she was unconscious for the decision. The follow-on tension is already visible: she left without being able to say thank you properly, and she wounded [[Darby Stonefeather]] while incapacitated. Watch whether she treats being helped as a debt.
- **[[Darby Stonefeather]] is accumulating small humiliations.** Snubbed by Kit, pouting, then webbed on a natural 1 with her pet watching — Austin played all three as a single bad day and named it. Combined with her S23 material (introverted, 20 years old, dreads leading alone, refused inheritance), she is the character most likely to need an actual conversation soon. **Two unaddressed frictions with Kit now stack: the [[Indigo]] warning (S21) and the snub (S24).**
- **[[Aeolus]] keeps winning by refusing to fight.** The eldritch blast was his *second* idea. Addison rewarded the first idea with a threatened bard level and a 27, and then gave him the closing line of the session. This is now a stable pattern for the character and Addison is actively reinforcing it.
- **The bastion has become a rules subsystem the DM is learning in public.** Addison said so twice — *"I'm learning this as I read"* and *"we might need to spend some time working on it, but for now, I trust my improv."* Several things are explicitly owed in writing: the self-healing walls, [[Clementine]]'s statblock, the gold-orange fungus, and the axe beak hippogriff's terms. **Expect S25 to open with mechanical cleanup.**
- **Addison is planting a vertical.** The deep well *"drops to the next level down of the underdark,"* emanates something *"hostile and dark and abysmal,"* and is ringed with the bones of small, medium and large beasts. It is the only location the merfolk explicitly refuse to enter, and the party explicitly declined it. It will not stay declined.
- **[[Amanita de'Champignon]] hit a hard wall for the first time.** The closing fungus is deliberately outside her control, at exactly the moment the campaign has established her as the most powerful problem-solver at the table. Placing it at the cliffhanger is a deliberate move.
- **Table-safety practice is worth recording.** Addison paused a heavy scene unprompted to run a content check and offered to *"tamp things down"*; [[Kit Aluri]] had a pre-prepared alternate dream ready to swap in; the table consented explicitly. This is table practice, not a house rule, but it belongs in `00-Campaign-Hub/House Rules & Rulings.md` as one — **Taylor's call.**
