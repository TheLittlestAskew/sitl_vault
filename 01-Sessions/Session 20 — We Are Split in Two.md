## Character POV Overview

### Kit Aluri — POV Overview

**Day unknown — four days gone, then no days at all.**

We said goodbye to the boy first. Four days back down the road, before the river took the ground out from under us — I keep circling back to it now that we're standing still again. He'd earned his gear the hard way and he was leaving to spend it in a city that would kill him for the wrong accent. I did the thing soldiers do at a goodbye: checked his straps, made him show me he knew which end of the crossbow was which, made him say the words back to me in the tongue he was still chewing through. He told me his teacher was at peace. He told me I was next in line for that kind of loyalty, whether I'd asked for it or not. I gave him the old handshake — grip to the elbow, like we do it back home — and told him I hoped our roads crossed again. I meant it. I don't say that to many people.

Then the water took us and spat us out somewhere else — tired, waterlogged, and surrounded before we'd caught a full breath. I stood up on the wreck of my raft because sitting down felt like dying, and I yelled every language I own into the dark at once, common stacked on goblin stacked on the thieves' hand-code, because I didn't know which one would land and I didn't have the patience to guess politely. *Don't test me right now.* I put a bolt in the one swimming fastest at us before he got close enough to matter. It bought us exactly as much time as it needed to.

They took us anyway. Walked us in at spear-point through a wall of netting and old bone hooks, and I let them, because fighting a whole flooded city on no sleep is a soldier's way of dying for nothing. I looked the one who'd thrown my crewmate to the ground dead in his flat black eyes and told him, quiet, that the next time he touched one of mine he'd lose the hand that did it. He believed me. Good.

And then — of all the faces to find in a drowned, hostile town — his. Blue skin, green hair like weed on the tide, a trident he still knows how to use. I hadn't let myself wonder whether I'd see him again, because wondering costs you when the answer's no. I got the answer today, and it cost me nothing but a breath I didn't know I was holding.

The priest here wants us as bait for his own daughter. Wants us walked in as prisoners so he can put a knife through his own church's other half. I've done worse things for stranger reasons, and it turns out I've got a friend on the inside now — a small, ugly, clever, wet friend who fed me lies once in a cage and is, against every soldier's instinct in me, still a friend. He looked sorry when he said it. I told him survival isn't a debt. We both got out. That's the whole ledger, closed.

There's a man in this town who's given up on saving it — sits with his eyes open and his mind somewhere else, says his words are wasted breath either way. I don't think he's wrong, exactly. I think he's tired in a way I recognize, and I don't have room to judge a man for putting his sword down when I still haven't found a way to put mine down myself. Maybe that's the difference between us. Maybe it isn't.

So: we go in as prisoners again. I've done that before, too. Last time, it cost me almost everything and taught me who was worth carrying out the other side. I'd like to think I already know the answer here. I'm about to find out if I'm right.

## Session Analysis

### Narrative Summary

The session opens with Addison recapping the tail end of [[Session 18 — Trust the Whirlpool]] — the river run, the disguised green hag, [[Sloopidoop]]'s sacrifice-play against two more hags, and the party's whirlpool arrival at the edge of [[Sloobludop]] — largely for the benefit of [[Aeolus]]'s player, who had not yet joined the call (delayed train). Some quick housekeeping follows: [[Kit Aluri]] confirms she is the only party member to have leveled up so far (to 5, via the milestone at the end of S18); the rest of the party has been given DM-granted agency to level whenever feels narratively right, rather than all at once. [[Darby Stonefeather]] jokes about needing a "fighter's pep talk" and outlines her level-5 [[Path of the Wild Heart|Wild Heart]] "large form" option (extra reach, 55 ft. of movement) without actually leveling in-session.

To buy real-world time for Aeolus's player to arrive, Addison then runs a **flashback to four days earlier**, at the [[Fungal Cavern (Day 4)|Fungal Cavern]] campsite, covering the party's farewell to **[[Artaxle]]** before he splits off alone toward [[Menzoberranzan]]. [[Amanita de'Champignon]] gifts him a packet of dual-purpose mushrooms (a beneficial half, and a "cause a little indigestion" half). [[Kit Aluri]] and [[Darby Stonefeather]] both say their goodbyes; Artaxle expresses grief over the death of his mentor **[[Jorlan Duskryn]]** and gratitude to Kit for helping him process it. A lore dump follows: Kit and Binks roll history to learn about **[[Bregan D'aerthe]]** (the drow-majority-but-multiracial mercenary company Artaxle hopes to join) and its leader **[[Jarlaxle]]**, "One-Eyed Jack," who is confirmed to have worked both with and against **[[Drizzt Do'Urden]]** in the past. Kit also reveals — via a private roll and conversation — a personal contact of hers with loose Bregan D'aerthe ties, last known to be operating out of the [[The Darklake|Dark Lake]]/[[Mantol-Derith]] region; she asks Artaxle to use that name if he runs into trouble. The party outfits Artaxle with drow gear, rations, and a final gear check before he departs alone, on foot, toward Menzoberranzan.

Back in the present, the party is regrouping in open water after the whirlpool: [[Derendil]] is now conscious but flailing (still exhausted, no memory of the past several hours), and nearly everyone (Amanita, Binks, Blarg, Turvy, Stool) is confirmed at one point of exhaustion via con saves — only [[Topsy]] passes clean. Through the murk, [[Amanita de'Champignon]] and [[Darby Stonefeather]] catch garbled Undercommon words ("human," "whirlpool") as a hostile-looking contingent of nine kuo-toa — one clearly a leader, backed by eight others — swims toward the party at speed. With two rounds' warning, the party scrambles: [[Kit Aluri]] stands on her raft and shouts warnings/threats in every language she knows (Common, Goblin, Thieves' Cant, and halting Undercommon, boosted by Second Wind), then puts a crossbow bolt into the lead kuo-toa (17 to-hit, 9 damage). [[Darby Stonefeather]] and [[Roxy]] make slow progress toward shore through difficult water; **[[Topsy]]** lands a poisoned hand crossbow bolt (15 to-hit, 7 damage) on one of the escorting kuo-toa guards, who fails its save by more than 5 and is knocked unconscious. **Aeolus** — narrated/rolled by the DM — lands an Eldritch Blast (18 to-hit, 13 damage) on the kuo-toa leader/"monitor." [[Blarg]] (as a water-adapted DM-run NPC) drags Kit most of the way to shore by rope.

Once ashore, [[Binks Stonevein]] talks the situation down: using Thaumaturgy to project his voice, he apologizes for the intrusion (Persuasion with disadvantage, ~23 after exhaustion), invokes [[Sloopidoop]]'s name and the token he was given, and later backs it with a second Persuasion roll (21, aided by Heroic Inspiration) that gets two of the four remaining kuo-toa guards to stand down. The party is escorted in — hostile, but not fighting — through the settlement's gate: a wall of heavy netting on Zurkhwood posts, studded with bone hooks. [[Kit Aluri]] spots a fleeing deep-blue humanoid figure near the docks (Perception 15, Insight 19) and correctly identifies the skin tone as belonging to her ally **[[Indigo]]**, a Triton she knows from [[Mantol-Derith]] — though she says nothing to the party yet.

Inside the gate, the party is suddenly flanked by **two** kuo-toa factions at once: the group escorting them, and a second, larger group approaching led by a kuo-toa in robes like [[Sloopidoop]]'s, accompanied by **Indigo** himself, armed and unreadable. On a signal, Indigo's group **ambushes the original escorts, not the party** — [[Binks Stonevein]] casts Invisibility to slip away, but is caught anyway (the kuo-toa here can sense invisible creatures — new tactical information); [[Kit Aluri]]'s Intimidation (22) drops one hostile whip, who is finished off by a friendly kuo-toa blade from behind. In minutes, the tide turns completely: the party's original captors are wiped out or fled, replaced by an allied faction. Their leader, using Tongues so everyone can understand him, introduces himself as **[[Ploop|Ploopploopeen — "call me Ploop"]]**, current Archpriest of the Sea Mother (**[[Blibdoolpoolp]]**, whose name is deliberately garbled in-fiction), and asks for the party's help — "help us and you will be rewarded."

Ploop explains that [[Sloobludop]] is in the middle of a religious civil war: his own daughter, **[[Aplopod]]**, experienced a vision of a new god — **Limu Gugun, "the Deep Father"** — weeks ago, and has since drawn away a large and growing faction of followers, who make human/humanoid blood sacrifices and "chum" the Dark Lake for something unknown that consumes it. Ploop confirms **Sloopidoop** was his predecessor as Archpriest, gone "some months" on Society of Brilliance business, and sends scouts (naming the hag **Nanny Plunk** as a known danger) to check on his condition after Kit mentions the party's recent encounter with him. Ploop's son, **Glug**, a hostile "whip," objects loudly to outsiders' involvement but is overruled by his father.

The party is led through Sloobludop's stilt-built, multi-level, distinctly fish-reeking streets (wealth correlating with proximity to the waterline) to the **Shrine of the Sea Mother** — a nine-foot idol built from wood, a giant crayfish's head and claws, and offerings of stones, mushrooms, and rotting fish. Kit's high Religion check (21) reveals the Sea Mother is a **scavenger goddess** who values discarded/personal offerings, including regurgitated ones; several kuo-toa demonstrate by vomiting at the statue's feet. The party makes its own offerings: Kit throws in an old bracer; Amanita offers scavenged crawfish and glowing spores (Religion/Nature 10, no special boon but visible approval from onlookers); and — at Darby's suggestion — **Roxy**, prompted by a botched Animal Handling check (3), defecates in front of the shrine instead of vomiting. Darby owns it ("I'm a farmer, I can handle it") and successfully reframes it as fertilizer (12), earning genuine approval from Ploop ("a fine offering indeed from such a noble creature") and an audible, disembodied feminine giggle from the shrine itself.

Meanwhile, **Indigo** peels off with Kit — using thieves' cant to relay, cryptically, that a contact of Artaxle's was seen heading toward Menzoberranzan and is "ragged but" alive — and invites her to a hidden tavern reserved for non-kuo-toa, kept open at personal risk by its keeper (a Sea Mother loyalist). Kit slips away from the group unnoticed (staying in telepathic contact via Amanita's spores) while the rest of the party spends roughly an hour with Ploop planning the operation. At the tavern, Kit reunites with two faces from her past captivity: **[[Shuushar]]**, deep in meditation, aloof and neutral ("if you live, you live; if you die, you die — I will do what I will do," though he offers passive aid from the water if needed), and **[[Jimjar]]**, alive and disguised in kuo-toa netting, revealed to be running an intelligence network with ties to **Bregan D'aerthe** lieutenants in the northeast passages. Kit and Jimjar reconcile without rancor over his past betrayal ("survival is survival... no hard feelings"). Jimjar warns that a Deep Father contingent was seen heading toward the Archpriest's home minutes ago — **to arrest Ploop**.

The session's final scene cuts back to the rest of the party at Ploop's home just as that arrest attempt arrives: a masculine voice outside, not Aplopod herself, demands Ploop's surrender and escort "to the altar of the Deep Father." Ploop turns to the party and asks, as planned, whether they'll go in as bait. **[[Binks Stonevein]]** ("I love trickery — yes, I'll do it") and **[[Amanita de'Champignon]]** agree eagerly; the group steps outside together, Binks leaving one bystander with a cryptic parting whisper ("it was the little statue"), and the session ends there — mid-cliffhanger, before the ambush plan is executed.

### Themes & Emotional Beats

- **Fractured families, chosen ones:** Sloobludop's civil war is a father-vs-daughter (and father-vs-son) schism — Ploop's own children stand on opposite sides of him. This directly mirrors and inverts the party's own found-family arc: where the party has built loyalty across strangers (Kit and Darby in S18; Kit and Jimjar and Indigo this session), Ploop's *actual* blood family is what's tearing his community apart.
- **Reconciliation without erasure:** Kit's reunion with Jimjar is the session's clearest character beat — she doesn't pretend his past betrayal didn't happen, but explicitly separates "survival" from "hard feelings." Paired with the equally warm, wordless reunion with Indigo, it continues S18's trust-earning arc into new relationships rather than just the existing party.
- **Faith performed vs. faith felt:** The shrine scene plays the mechanics of worship for broad comedy (a goliath mount's dung passed off as a "noble" offering) right alongside a genuinely eerie, unscripted response — the shrine's disembodied giggle — suggesting the Sea Mother's attention is real and active, not just set dressing.
- **Prisoners again, on purpose:** Kit's closing internal note (see POV Overview) draws an explicit throughline from the party's original captivity to this session's voluntary return to "prisoner" status — this time chosen, as tactics, rather than suffered.
- **Neutral parties and the cost of not choosing:** Shuushar's total non-engagement ("if you live, you live... I will do what I will do") stands in sharp contrast to Ploop's and Indigo's active risk-taking, offering the table a third model of how NPCs navigate the region's chaos.
